## 1. Placing it on the site

1. Unzip into `/games/outer-line/` on the Trip and Route site. The folder is self-contained:

   | File | Use |
   |---|---|
   | `index.html` | the game page |
   | `play.html` | the game; the page embeds it |
   | `ride.jpg` | hero still (real play) |
   | `map.jpg` | spot-select still |
   | `beanies.jpg` | Scramble's locker with all nine beanies earned |
   | `card-16x9.jpg` | 1280×720 grid card for the games listing |
   | `og.jpg` | 1200×630 link preview |
   | `icon-512.png` | app/tile icon |
   | `game.json` | listing metadata for the portal grid |

2. In `index.html`, make the `og:image` URL absolute (`https://<site>/games/outer-line/og.jpg`).
3. Add the grid tile from `game.json`. Use `card` as the image, `title`, and `short` as the line under it. The link goes to `/games/outer-line/`.
4. Add the page to the sitemap.
5. Smoke test after upload:
   - the page loads
   - "Drop in" plays inside the frame on a laptop and full screen on a phone
   - "Today's route" starts the daily ride
   - ↑ jumps

**How the page embeds the game:**
- It fetches `play.html` and inlines it (srcdoc). If the fetch fails, it falls back to `play.html?play=1` or `?daily=1`.
- While the game is on screen, the page passes game keys (arrows, Space, WASD, Esc) into it. Pressing ↑ never scrolls the page instead of jumping. Once the game is scrolled out of view, the keys scroll the page again.

If the site wraps game pages in its own template, keep the `<iframe>` approach. The game needs the full frame, and keyboard focus has to be able to reach it.

## 2. The game in one breath

**Outer Line** (working title), a Trip and Route game. You're Scramble, an egg on a skateboard, skating the space metro after hours. Carve the tunnel, step onto a wall when the floor runs out, and chain wall rides, gaps and hops into a line that keeps getting faster.

- **Tagline:** Walls are floors. Tricks are speed.
- **Daily line:** One route a day. Same for everyone.
- **Short (≤ 80):** Scramble, an egg on a skateboard, in the space metro. Tricks are speed.
- **Medium:** You're Scramble, an egg on a skateboard, skating the space metro after hours. Carve the tunnel, step onto a wall when the floor runs out, and chain wall rides, gaps and hops into a line that keeps getting faster. One route a day, the same for everyone.
- **Tags:** skate, runner, tunnel, momentum, daily, egg.
- **Controls:**
  - keyboard: ← → carve, **↑ jump** (Space or W also work; hold in the air to glide), ↓ push (tuck on a downhill), Esc for the spots
  - touch: hold a side to carve, tap or swipe up to jump, hold both to tuck
- **Status:** browser prototype. 10 spots plus the daily route.
- **Rating notes:** no chat, no accounts, no ads. Nothing leaves the device; progress and the day's result sit in localStorage.

## 3. How it plays

- **Momentum is the game.** A flow meter (0–1) sets speed: base × (1 + 0.8 × flow), up to 1.8× the base speed (2.07× while tucked).
  - Tricks fill it. It drains slowly, 70 % slower while a combo is live, and a fall empties it.
  - Speed and lens ease toward the new value over about a quarter second, so a trick feels like a surge rather than a jolt.
- **Tricks:**

  | Trick | How | Flow | Pts |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Wall ride | roll onto another side (0.5 s cooldown) | .07 | 100 |
  | Gap | clear a gap row | .10 | 200 |
  | Big gap | clear 5+ rows in one jump | .16 | 400 |
  | Transfer | change sides in the air | .10 | 250 |
  | Hop | jump again within 0.16 s of landing after real air | .05 | 80 |
  | Edge | ride a hole's edge for 0.18 s+ | .05 | 150 |

  - Combos chain within 2.4 s, with the score multiplier capped at ×8.
  - Tiers go ROLLING → FLOWING → FULL SEND, with callouts and combo sounds that climb a scale.
- **Steering is slippery.** Sideways speed builds and slides out instead of snapping between lanes. Turning against your slide bites harder, and grip is lower in the air.
- **The rider: Scramble**
  - a smooth green egg in a beanie, on a lime skateboard that points down the tunnel and turns up to about 20° into a carve
  - squashes on landings and tucks, stretches with arms up in the air
  - casts a shadow on the floor when airborne, so you can read where you'll land
- **Nine beanies to earn.** Scramble starts with the OG. The rest unlock from riding, never from buying or grinding time, and each locked one says how to get it:

  | Beanie | Look | How |
  |---|---|---|
  | OG | magenta | from day one |
  | Lime Rib | lime, ribbed | land Dockyard |
  | Big Pom | cyan, big white pom | land 3 spots |
  | Night Bus | navy, yellow stripe | land 5 spots |
  | Finish Flag | checkered | land Terminus |
  | Full Send | orange with flames | hit FULL SEND |
  | Combo Cap | rainbow stripes | a ×20 combo |
  | Daily Moon | indigo with a moon | land today's route on the first ride |
  | Gold | gold with a shine | 100,000 style in one ride |

  - A new beanie calls out mid-ride ("NEW BEANIE: FULL SEND") and shows on the arrival or daily card.
  - The locker on the spot screen equips them. The choice is saved on the device.
  - Riders who had already landed spots get those progress beanies on their next visit.
- **Camera:**
  - The tunnel rolls so the wall you're on becomes the floor. The roll eases in across each wall (a damped spring with feed-forward), so changing walls is a glide, not a snap.
  - Landing shake is a smooth wobble.
  - A fall slows down smoothly, spins the egg up from rest, and dips to black for the restart.
- **Forever mode** (from the studio, 2026-10-26): a third button on the spot screen and the page.
  - One endless line, built ahead as you ride, with a random seed each run.
  - Over 2,400 rows it ramps from Dockyard-easy to meaner than Terminus: holes 10 → 72 %, path 3 → 1 tiles wide, gaps every 30 → 13 rows and 1 → 3 rows long, base speed 10 → 15.5.
  - One fall ends the run. The end card shows distance, style, top speed, best combo and your best; the best is saved, and the progress bar races it on the next run.
  - Links: `?forever=1`, or `window.__OL_START = "forever"`.
- **Push** (v13, founder: "can holding down make you go slightly faster, like skateboarding, pushing off"): hold ↓ (both sides on touch).
  - **On the flat or an uphill:** Scramble kicks off the ground with the back foot every 0.45 s, with a soft scrape. Each kick adds 0.08× base of carry, up to +0.22× base, so a rider with no flow goes about 20 % faster.
  - **First kick:** comes almost as soon as you press.
  - **On a downhill:** holding ↓ is still a tuck, which builds hill speed 2× faster. Tuck's grip penalty now applies only while actually tucked.
- **Fairer jumps and checkpoints** (v12, founder: "not rewarding … sometimes I just fall instead of jumping, there's a weird delay … a bit hard"):
  - **Coyote time:** a jump pressed up to 0.12 s after rolling off an edge still jumps. v8's strict holes had taken away the old forgiveness for late presses. With presses spread ±90 ms around the edge, falls went from 2.8 a minute to 0.
  - **Holding jump at an edge jumps:** so holding ↑ from a glide into the next edge no longer drops you.
  - **Checkpoints:** every gap landing is a checkpoint on spots and practice. A fall respawns you there, with style and coins kept and half your flow, instead of sending you back to the start. The arrival card counts falls. The daily's first ride and Forever stay one-fall.
  - **Touch, not changed:** a quick tap jumps on release, up to 0.17 s after touch-down, which is part of the "delay" on phones. Coyote time absorbs most of it. Jumping on touch-down would clash with hold-to-steer, so that's a controls decision for the founder.
- **Pace** (v11): founder rated v10 "80 out of 100, 68 would be perfect". One global factor, `PACE = 0.85` (68/80), scales all forward motion: base speed, flow, downhill carry and the top speed. Everything keeps its proportions. Crest launches compare against the paced base, so they trigger as before. Layouts are unchanged, and only more forgiving at the lower speed. Measured with the human-like rider: 1.77× → 1.54× base on average (87 %). One number to tune later: `PACE`.
- **Glide, coins, bigger hills** (v10, founder: "even faster downhill, more air off crests, glide mode with coins too, remix dopamine dings from Bell Theory"; glide chosen as "hold to glide"):
  - **Faster downhill:** speed builds 2.1× faster than v9's start (SLOPE_G 55, tucked ×2). A downhill can take you a full 1× base past the flat top, for 3.07× base. Layouts are rebuilt for that top again, so hole patterns and today's daily changed once more.
  - **Crest air:** launches of 4–11 height and up to 13 rows long (were 3–8 and 8 rows). A crest still never lands you in a gap.
  - **Glide:** in the air, keep holding ↑ / Space / W (on touch, hold both sides) and Scramble spreads out and falls slowly.
    - It lasts up to 0.8 s per air and costs speed. A held glide stretches a plain jump from 4.1 to 7.2 rows.
    - It only works after a jump or crest, so rolling off an edge can't be rescued.
    - A hint explains it the first time you jump.
  - **Coins:** an arc over every jump-gap, a high glide line off every crest (only reachable with speed and glide), and rows down some downhills. They count in the HUD (◎) and on the arrival and Forever cards, and add style and a little flow.
  - **Dings:** Bell Theory's coin sound, remixed. A triangle blip sweeps up 1.6× over a soft low thump. Here it climbs a pentatonic ladder as coins chain within 0.7 s, every fifth coin adds a little arpeggio, and each coin group plays Bell Theory's quiet high "glint" when it comes into view.
- **Stronger hills** (v9, founder: "make the hills feel stronger, more speed downhill"):
  - **Speed:** downhills build speed 1.5× faster (SLOPE_G 26 → 40), and tucking downhill builds it 1.8× faster (was 1.5×).
  - **Top speed:** a downhill can now take you 0.6× base past the flat-ground top, so 2.67× base instead of 2.07×.
  - **Feel:** the view drops more (VIEW_TILT 0.8 → 1.2), the lens widens twice as much with carry, and speed lines double and brighten.
  - **Measured:** a human-like rider (late reactions, aim noise, varied jump timing) averages 1.66× base instead of 1.54×. The test bot tops out at 2.47–2.58×.
  - **Cost:** layouts are built for the higher top speed (more room left for sideways steps), so every spot and daily route has a slightly different hole pattern than v8, and today's daily changed mid-day.
- **Holes are holes** (v8, founder: "with enough speed you can just glide over the holes … basically cheatable"):
  - **The bug:** rolling off an edge started a fall from floor height, and you could land anywhere down to 0.35 below the floor. Above about 1.4× base speed you crossed a one-row hole before sinking that far (0 falls in 12 at each of 1.4×, 1.8× and 2.07× base); slower, you fell. That's why it was sometimes.
  - **Now:** roll off an edge without jumping and you get 0.03 s to be back over floor, enough for a carve that brushes a hole's side and too short for any forward crossing. After that, the far edge is a lip you hit. It's a fall at every speed (12 of 12 at each), and jumps still clear holes.
  - **Kept:** a jump that comes up a little short can still catch the lip, as before.
- **Hills** (v7, founder: "downhill and uphill variations, so momentum has more use, but slowing down is rare"):
  - **Carry:** downhills build carry, extra speed on top of flow, and tucking on a downhill builds it 1.5× faster. It bleeds off slowly on the flat, and an uphill spends it before it touches your speed.
  - **Uphills:** they only follow a downhill, and each is sized from the speed that downhill actually gave (at most 60 % of it), so you crest still rolling. Measured: riders spend 0 % of the time below their flow speed on every spot, even with no flow at all.
  - **Speed cap:** hills lift you toward each spot's designed top speed (full flow, tucked) but never past it, so every layout and daily route is unchanged.
  - **Crests:** top a hill fast (1.25× base or more) and you launch, for a new CREST trick. A crest never lands you in a gap.
  - **Jump length:** a jump now covers at most 8 rows. Fast, it's a lower, flatter ollie. This fixes a real bug: at top speed one jump on Terminus carried over a gap into the next.
  - **How hills show:**
    - the whole view drops on a downhill and lifts on an uphill
    - the tunnel bends where the slope changes
    - a ▼ DOWNHILL / ▲ UPHILL readout sits under the speed
    - downhills bring more speed lines and a wider lens, and Scramble crouches
  - **Gaps stay visible:** in the 10 rows before a jump-gap, through the gap and its landing, the drawn slope never bends over, so no hill hides a gap.
  - **Share of each ride:** 22–56 % downhill and up to 13 % uphill, by spot.
- **Gaps you can see** (v6, founder: "it's hard to see the gaps"). The real cause was contrast: the floor was about rgb(25,25,35) and a hole showed the near-black background, about 1.1:1, so a hole looked like a slightly darker tile.
  - The floor is now a lit surface: grey tinted with the spot colour, brightest on the side you ride, and raised per spot until it's at least 4:1 against black.
  - Holes are filled solid black. Tiles are drawn edge to edge, so the dark seams that used to look like small holes are gone.
  - Measured on rendered frames, a hole against the floor tile beside it is 3.5:1 on Dockyard, 3.9:1 on Lantern Market and 3.8:1 on Comet Yard (from about 1.1:1).
  - Side hole edges are now red only on the floor you ride, so the magenta spots no longer blur into red.
- **Gap markers** (from the studio): the row before every jump-gap is tinted red with a thick red lip, and the landing row is tinted lime. Single holes get a faint red outline. Edges next to holes light red, loudest on the floor you're riding.
- **Look:** lime, cyan and magenta on black, heavy italic type, tape labels, skewed bars and offset shadows. Effects-only sound, no background music bed, with a mute button.

## 4. Positioning against Run 3

Run 3 (Joseph Cloutier, 2014) is the title Coolmath's 2025 top-10 ranks first: a space tunnel runner where walls become floors. Outer Line competes on what Run 3 doesn't do:

| | Run 3 | Outer Line |
|---|---|---|
| Core reward | survive, reach the end | momentum: tricks make you faster |
| Character | grey alien runner | Scramble, an egg on a skateboard, with beanies to earn |
| Phone play | keyboard-first | built for both |
| Session | long level map | 20–40 s spot-to-spot rides |
| Coming back | unlock levels | one shared route a day, a share card with a style score, no streak |
| Weight | large game | one 49 kB file, instant on a school Chromebook |

**Rule:** never name, show or imitate Run 3 in anything we publish. The genre is open; the specific game isn't. Organic posts can let players make the comparison themselves.

**Second rule:** Scramble is a generic green egg. "Big Green Egg" is a registered trademark (a kamado grill brand). Don't use that name, its logo, or a grill look in the game, on the page, or in posts.

## 8. Decisions for the founder

0. **Hill feel.** The numbers are tuned so nobody slows down and no gap hides. How strong hills feel is a matter of taste: `SLOPE_G` (speed gained per slope), `VIEW_TILT` (how far the view drops) and `HILL_VIS` (how much the tunnel bends) are the dials. The bend is deliberately subtle, because a strong bend hid gaps and holes in testing.
0. **The lit floor.** Gaps are now unmistakable, but the tunnel reads brighter and flatter than the old near-black look. If it feels too bright, `FLOOR_MIN_LUM` (now .15) can come down to about .1 and still keep 3:1.
1. **Name.** "Outer Line" is a working title. Check the domain and handles, and run a trademark search before going public.
2. **Feel by hand.** Play spots 1–10 on a laptop and a phone. Is the slide fun? Is FULL SEND too easy to keep? Those answers set the next tuning pass.
3. **Scramble.** The name is my pick. It's a common word; Konami's 1981 arcade game is also called *Scramble*, which is fine for a character name but worth a glance in the trademark search. Boards (deck colours) are the natural next unlockable.
4. **Daily attempts.** First ride counts now. Keep that, or best-of-three?
5. **Where it lives.** Move `games/outer-line/` to the studio repo or its own, so Bell Theory's repo stays Bell Theory's.
6. **Measurement.** Nothing is counted. A cookieless count of daily plays per date would show whether people come back.
